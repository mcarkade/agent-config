# Source-faithful prototype

1. Map the source parts, assembly method, owned inventory and machine limits before changing geometry. Preserve the source look and load path. Record each necessary departure and its reason.
2. Make parts a builder would handle. Merge adjacent skin faces into formable wraps; split members only when stock or the cutter requires it. Count real pieces, joints and operations. Prefer local fitting over a new architecture.
3. Use existing hardware and simple accessible joints where they carry the load. Do not design around unmeasured future hardware. State what must be measured before final fitting.
4. Release one numbered cutting and printing set with a short illustrated assembly sequence, placement, every electrical connection and checks. Show intermediate fits, not just the finished model.
5. Compare every final part with its source, especially after booleans. Prove exact cut, print and guide files by readback and hashes. Label predictions as predictions; physical fit, strength, balance and flight remain real-world checks.
