# Physics Explained visual grammar (from frame study of Bzo-0ybo3do and SGUMC19IISY, Nov 2026)

- Pure black canvas, 16:9. No logos, no backgrounds, no slide chrome.
- Equations: Cambria Math look, warm white, full-size (display) fractions, generous spacing around = and +.
- Captions: small clean sans (Segoe-like). A short white label sits above an equation ("Newton's Second Law").
  Explanatory pairs in soft peach: "If we know x, y ...  ->  these equations tell us how those quantities change".
- Logic glyphs: thin teal arrows between statements; thin teal box around key results; red ellipse or red
  strike-through on the term being discussed or cancelled.
- Equations grow term by term on the same line in sync with narration; finished lines move up or stay;
  full clear between ideas. Do NOT dim earlier lines: the user found dimming odd; finished lines stay or leave on a clear.
- One new element every 2-3 s; transitions are short fades; layout persists so the eye knows where to look.
- Worked numerics: values written into the formula, then "= -2.041" appended; results collected in a top row.
- Diagrams: thin-line plots (light blue/pink curves, white axes, no ticks), animated reveal; simple disc
  objects (sun, planet), arrows for velocity/acceleration; occasional photos/portraits.
- Narration: first person, plain, reads what is on screen and justifies each step ("Now notice...",
  "So we see..."). Questions he poses ("What happens if we keep the next term?") are put on screen as text.
- Videos 30-60 min, no filler inside the content.
